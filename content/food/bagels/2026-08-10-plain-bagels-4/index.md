>[!IMPORTANT]
>**This post was retroactively written. For completeness of my bagel journey I tried my best to create this log entry from scattered notes well after the fact.**

Back to plain dough to work on the fundamentals. This batch used a longer cold ferment for more flavor development, and bagel boards for the first phase of the bake.

## What I did
350 g total flour weight, with vital wheat gluten bringing the protein content up from King Arthur bread flour's 12.7%.

**As weights:**
- 342.5 g bread flour
- 7.5 g vital wheat gluten
- 17.5 g barley malt syrup
- 5.25 g salt
- 1.4 g instant yeast
- 185.5 g cold water

**As baker percentages:**
- 97.87% bread flour
- 2.13% vital wheat gluten
- 5% barley malt syrup
- 1.5% salt
- 0.4% instant yeast
- 53% cold water

### Stage 1
1. Whisk bread flour and vital wheat gluten together
2. Dissolve barley malt syrup in cold water
3. Add wet to dry and mix until a shaggy dough forms
4. Add salt and yeast
5. Knead until the dough passes a window test
6. Divide and rest 5 minutes
7. Shape into bagels
8. Place into fridge for cold ferment, roughly 36 hours

### Stage 2
9. Remove bagels from fridge and let come to room temperature
10. Float test
11. Preheat oven to 450F
12. Boil each bagel
13. Apply everything seasoning immediately after boiling
14. Bake 6 minutes on bagel boards
15. Flip onto silicone sheet and bake 10 minutes
16. Rotate baking sheet and bake another 10 minutes, turning the oven off for the last 5 to keep the everything seasoning from burning

## How it went
The bagels were in the fridge for roughly 36 hours. After about an hour out of the fridge they still had not passed the float test, though they did float in the boiling water after 10 to 15 seconds. Floating in the bath rather than in cold water is a sign of under-proofing, since the heat accelerates the remaining yeast activity and expands the existing gas.

Turning the oven off for the last 5 minutes was an attempt to keep the everything seasoning from scorching.

## Results
Good flavor and good color. The crust developed well.

The crumb was too dense. Some of that comes from the under-proofing, and some likely from the yeast quantity being too low for a 36 hour ferment at fridge temperature.

## What I'd change next time
- Increase yeast percentage for a 36 hour cold ferment
- Target floating within 10 to 15 minutes of coming out of the fridge as the calibration signal
- Measure fridge temperature to make the yeast calculation more repeatable
