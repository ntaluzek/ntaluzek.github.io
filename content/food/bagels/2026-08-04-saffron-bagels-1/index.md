I'm a big fan of saffron rice so why not a saffron bagel? While I'm at it, what about bringing in saffron's common partners of cardamom and rose?

## What I did
Saffron was steeped in a portion of the recipe water that was heated, which extracts both the color and the flavor. That liquid was cooled fully before being combined with the rest of the cold water.

**As weights:**
- 500 g bread flour
- 280 g cold water total, with 60 g used for the saffron steep
- 20 g barley malt syrup
- 7.5 g salt
- 4 g instant yeast
- 1/2 tsp saffron threads, loosely packed
- 1/4 tsp freshly ground cardamom
- Splash of rose water

**As baker percentages:**
- 100% bread flour
- 56% water
- 4% barley malt syrup
- 1.5% salt
- 0.8% instant yeast

### Stage 1
1. Heat 60 g of the water to 80C, add saffron, and steep 20 minutes
2. Cool the saffron liquid completely, then combine with the remaining 220 g cold water
3. Whisk flour, salt, and yeast together in mixing bowl
4. Dissolve barley malt syrup in the saffron water
5. Add wet to dry and mix until a shaggy dough forms
6. Knead until smooth and elastic
7. Divide and rest 5 minutes
8. Shape into bagels
9. Place into fridge for cold ferment

### Stage 2
10. Remove bagels from fridge and let come to room temperature
11. Float test
12. Preheat oven to 410F, lower than usual since pistachio oils burn readily
13. Boil each bagel
14. Press nigella seeds and crushed pistachios onto the wet surface immediately after boiling
15. Bake, rotating baking sheet half way through

## Results
Lots of saffron flavor, and a nice yellow color. No cardamom or rose flavor came through, could increase those quantities and probably reduce the saffron.

## What I'd change next time
- More cardamom and rose water
- Perhaps less saffron so that the other flavors can come through more
