>**This post was retroactively written. For completeness of my bagel journey I tried my best to create this log entry from scattered notes well after the fact.**


After my success with the whole wheat bagel recipe that leveraged vital wheat gluten to ensure the right gluten content in the dough I thought that same technique could be used to use other "flours" that don't have any of their own gluten. Masa harina is nixtamalized corn flour used for making corn tortillas. This an attempt at a corn tortilla flavored bagel.

## What I did
30% masa substitution with vital wheat gluten added to compensate for the displaced gluten protein. Hydration raised to account for masa's absorption.

**As weights:**
- 330 g bread flour
- 150 g masa harina
- 35 g vital wheat gluten
- 20 g barley malt syrup
- 10 g salt
- 3 g instant yeast
- 310 g cold water

**As baker percentages:**
- 66% bread flour
- 30% masa harina
- 7% vital wheat gluten
- 4% barley malt syrup
- 2% salt
- 0.6% instant yeast
- 62% cold water

### Stage 1
1. Whisk bread flour, masa harina, and vital wheat gluten together
2. Add cold water and mix until a rough dough forms
3. Autolyse 30 minutes covered
4. Add barley malt syrup, salt, and yeast and work in
5. Knead 15-18 minutes
6. Divide and rest 5 minutes
7. Shape into bagels
8. Place into fridge for cold ferment

### Stage 2
9. Remove bagels from fridge and let come to room temperature
10. Float test
11. Preheat oven to 450F
12. Boil each bagel 60 seconds per side
13. Bake, rotating baking sheet half way through

## How it went
The bagels were cold fermented for around 14 hours. After coming out of the fridge they sat at room temperature for 50 minutes and did not pass the float test. I gave them more time and after over 2 hours out of the fridge they still had not passed.

I went ahead with the boil anyway. The bagels floated in the boiling water after about 20 seconds, which is a sign they were under-proofed rather than fully stalled. The heat of the boiling water accelerates whatever yeast activity is left and expands the existing gas, so floating in the bath is not the same as passing a cold water float test.

## Results
The bagels were noticeably salty, more than expected from a 2% salt level. Masa carries its own mineral character from the calcium hydroxide used in nixtamalization, which reads as a background saltiness. 

Other than the saltiness, these were awesome! Exactly what I hoped for in terms of the "corn tortilla" flavor coming through while still staying true to the bagel form factor and texture experience.

These were excellent topped with avocado. Makes me think that they could usher in a new age of bagel sandwiches with toppings like pickled red onion, guacamole, cilantro, and a black bean burger or mash.

## What I'd change next time
- Drop salt from 2%
- Add apple cider vinegar or a small amount of baking soda to correct for masa's residual alkalinity, which likely suppressed the yeast
- Increase yeast quantity to compensate for the acid or alkalinity suppression
- Test the pH of the dough before adding yeast
