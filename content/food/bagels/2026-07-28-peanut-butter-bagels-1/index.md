>**This post was retroactively written. For completeness of my bagel journey I tried my best to create this log entry from scattered notes well after the fact.**


The goal was a bagel that would be begging for a layer of jelly. Defatted peanut flour seemed like the right vehicle since it delivers concentrated peanut protein without the fat load that would shorten the gluten network. This is another experiment with the foundational theory that vital wheat gluten can be used to compensate for flour displacement that doesn't bring its own gluten content.

## What I did
20% substitution of defatted peanut flour with vital wheat gluten to compensate for the displaced gluten. Agave in place of barley malt syrup to push the flavor sweeter and avoid the savory malt character competing with the peanut.

**As weights:**
- 183 g bread flour
- 50 g defatted peanut flour
- 18 g vital wheat gluten
- 20 g agave nectar
- 5 g salt
- 1.5 g instant yeast
- 150 g cold water (plus 10 g extra added during mixing)

**As baker percentages:**
- 73% bread flour
- 20% defatted peanut flour
- 7% vital wheat gluten
- 4% agave nectar
- 1.5% salt
- 0.6% instant yeast
- 60% cold water

### Stage 1
1. Whisk bread flour, peanut flour, and vital wheat gluten together
2. Add water and agave, mix until combined
3. Knead
4. Divide and rest 5 minutes
5. Shape into bagels, with some having peanut butter rolled in as a strip
6. Place into fridge for cold ferment

### Stage 2
7. Remove bagels from fridge and let come to room temperature
8. Float test
9. Preheat oven to 450F
10. Boil each bagel
11. Bake, rotating baking sheet half way through

## How it went
Ended up adding roughly 10g more water than the recipe called for to bring the dough together.

Some of the bagels had dollops of peanut butter rolled into them during shaping, laid in like a sushi maki roll. The peanut butter sometimes fell out in small clumps, since there was nothing binding the filling to the surrounding dough.

## Results
Some peanut flavor came through but not enough to call it a peanut bagel.

The ones with peanut butter rolled in were really good. That was the clear standout from this batch and points toward fat being the thing carrying the actual peanut butter flavor.

## What I'd change next time
- Increase the peanut flour percentage
- Try PB2 or similar powder handled as a swirl, the way cinnamon sugar gets layered into a cinnamon raisin bagel
- Find a way to keep the peanut butter filling from falling out
