>**This post was retroactively written. For completeness of my bagel journey I tried my best to create this log entry from scattered notes well after the fact.**

Give me a bagel with the spicy and tangy flavor of a buffalo sauce but make sure it is in every bite!

## What I did
Frank's RedHot Buffalo sauce is essentially a vinegar based hot sauce with artificial butter flavor. That makes it easier to work into a dough than other sauces that have a fat component to them since the fat would shorten the gluten network. The tradeoff is a significant acid load. The buffalo sauce replaced a large portion of the water. Since the sauce is roughly 70% water, that water content was accounted for in the hydration target. The sauce also carries substantial sodium, enough that no additional salt was added at all.

**As weights:**
- 283 g King Arthur bread flour
- 17 g vital wheat gluten
- 65 g Frank's RedHot Buffalo sauce
- 9 g brown sugar
- 0.9 g instant yeast
- 0 g added salt
- 117 g cold water
- 0.5 g cayenne pepper

**As baker percentages:**
- 94.3% King Arthur bread flour
- 5.7% vital wheat gluten
- 21.7% Frank's RedHot Buffalo sauce
- 3% brown sugar
- 0.3% instant yeast
- 0.17% cayenne pepper
- 39% added water, for roughly 54% true hydration

### Stage 1
1. Whisk bread flour, vital wheat gluten, and cayenne together
2. Combine buffalo sauce, brown sugar, and water
3. Add wet to dry and mix until combined
4. Add instant yeast
5. Knead
6. Divide and rest 5 minutes
7. Shape into bagels
8. Place into fridge for cold ferment

### Stage 2
9. Remove bagels from fridge and let come to room temperature
10. Float test
11. Preheat oven
12. Boil each bagel
13. Apply additional buffalo sauce after boiling and again mid-bake
14. Bake, rotating baking sheet half way through

## How it went
The shaped bagels went into the fridge for roughly 40 hours. They showed no visible size change at all over that time. After coming out of the fridge they sat at room temperature for 2 hours with no signs of yeast activity and never passed the float test.

## Results
This was a fermentation failure rather than a baking problem. The most likely cause is the acid load. Frank's RedHot has a pH around 3.5 to 4.0, and at 65 g in a 300 g flour batch the acetic acid concentration was high enough that the flour's buffering capacity could not keep the dough pH in a range where yeast stayed viable. Yeast activity drops sharply below pH 4.5 and essentially stops below 4.0.

## What I'd change next time
- Add baking soda to the dry ingredients to neutralize some of the acidity before it contacts the yeast
- Increase yeast substantially so a larger starting population has more resilience against acid suppression
- Test the pH of the wet ingredients before mixing, targeting 5.0 to 5.5
- The liquid sauce component could be reduced and then I could use dry buffalo flavorings such as cayenne and smoked paprika to carry flavor without the acid and moisture load
