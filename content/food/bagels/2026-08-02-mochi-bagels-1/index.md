Can you make a bagel with a mochi-like flavor and texture layered on top of a normal bagel chew? Glutinous rice flour which is used for making mochi contains no gluten despite the name. This again aligns with my experimentation involving vital wheat gluten.

## What I did
30% glutinous rice flour substitution. Vital wheat gluten lower than the masa formula because the amylopectin gel contributes some structural cohesion of its own, unlike masa's more passive starch.

**As weights:**
- 330 g bread flour
- 150 g glutinous rice flour
- 20 g vital wheat gluten
- 20 g barley malt syrup
- 7.5 g salt
- 4 g instant yeast
- 295 g cold water

**As baker percentages:**
- 66% bread flour
- 30% glutinous rice flour
- 4% vital wheat gluten
- 4% barley malt syrup
- 1.5% salt
- 0.8% instant yeast
- 59% cold water

### Stage 1
1. Whisk bread flour, glutinous rice flour, and vital wheat gluten together
2. Add cold water and mix until a rough dough forms
3. Autolyse 20-25 minutes
4. Add barley malt syrup, salt, and yeast and work in
5. Knead with wet hands rather than floured, since the amylopectin makes the dough sticky
6. Divide and rest 5 minutes
7. Shape into bagels
8. Place into fridge for cold ferment

### Stage 2
9. Remove bagels from fridge and let come to room temperature
10. Float test
11. Preheat oven
12. Boil each bagel
13. Bake, rotating baking sheet half way through

## Results
They turned out fine as bagels but did not seem meaningfully different from an all-wheat bagel. The flavor was slightly skewed toward rice and the crumb was slightly denser, but overall it was a normal bagel experience rather than anything mochi-like.

The 30% substitution was not enough for the amylopectin to become a primary structural element. The wheat gluten network was dominant enough that it simply absorbed the amylopectin contribution without changing how the bagel ate.

## What I'd change next time
- Increase glutinous rice flour to 50% or higher so the amylopectin competes with the gluten network rather than being absorbed by it
- Pre-gelatinize a portion of the glutinous rice flour into a mochi paste before incorporating, so actual mochi gel is present in the dough rather than relying on gelatinization during boiling and baking
- Lower the oven temperature to preserve moisture in the amylopectin gel
- Consider a longer boil or a gentler simmer to develop the surface gelatinization more fully
